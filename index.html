01
Where it started

Before the
code, there
was a broken
radio.

I was eight when I took apart a radio — not to fix it, but to see how sound could live inside wires. Curiosity shaped most of my early years.

By high school, it had become something more focused: a desire to build technology that felt useful and human. I learned that leadership and engineering both depend on clear communication. The quality of a system is often defined by the quality of communication within it.

Engineering felt like the cleanest way to turn curiosity into something useful.

Stellar Analytics — NASA Kepler Dataset

My team Brahmastra entered Stellar Analytics at IIT BHU's Technex with an ML pipeline for exoplanet transit classification — LightGBM, voting ensembles, and a neural network regressor. We hit ROC-AUC 0.9504 and R² of 0.9635, then took first place.

Quant Finance · AlgoZen

Building a Trading System I'd Actually Trust

AlgoZen wasn't built in a weekend. It went through multiple architectural rebuilds — regime detection, walk-forward optimization, genetic algorithm tuning on NIFTY 500. The targets were specific: Sharpe above 1.0, CAGR 14–18%, max drawdown under 15%. When it finally hit Sharpe 1.129, I understood why quantitative people are obsessive.

→ GitHub

Python · Regime Detection · Walk-forward Optimization · GA Tuning · NIFTY 500

Geospatial AI · ShadowMap

Predicting Urban Heat Before It Happens

ShadowMap began as an attempt to understand why some parts of a city feel physically harsher than others. I built a geospatial ML pipeline using Sentinel-3 LST, Landsat NDVI, and OpenStreetMap data to predict block-level urban heat patterns across Delhi. The challenge wasn't just accuracy — it was making climate data interactive, interpretable, and useful.

→ Live | → GitHub

XGBoost · FastAPI · React · Leaflet.js · Geospatial ML · Quantile Regression

03
Machine learning & quantitative thinking

Patterns
in the
noise.

Moving from physical systems to intelligent ones felt like natural gravity. The most important problems are already being measured — they're just not understood well enough yet.

ML gave me a different kind of engineering: one where you define the space for rules to emerge rather than defining every rule. Quant finance pulled on the same thread — making better decisions under uncertainty.

Right now I'm exploring diffusion models for exoplanet signal denoising, which is a very specific sentence that would have meant nothing to me two years ago and now feels like the obvious next step.
