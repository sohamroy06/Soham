Quant Finance · AlgoZen

AlgoZen
A Trading System I'd Actually Trust

A quantitative trading system for NIFTY 500 — built, broken, and rebuilt until it survived validation it wasn't designed to game.

Role Solo — Quant Developer
Targets Sharpe > 1.0 · CAGR 14–18% · Max Drawdown < 15%
Links → GitHub
01
The problem

Most retail-built trading strategies fail the moment they leave the backtest that produced them — they're overfit to history, or they assume market conditions that don't hold for more than a quarter. I wanted to build something with a real bar to clear: profitable, statistically defensible, and honest about how it would behave outside the data it was trained on.

02
Approach

AlgoZen went through several full architectural rebuilds before I trusted it. The strategy uses regime detection so its behavior adapts to different market conditions instead of assuming one regime holds forever, walk-forward optimization so every parameter is validated on data the model hasn't seen rather than fitted to the whole history at once, and a genetic algorithm to tune hyperparameters across a search space too large to grid-search by hand.

I set explicit targets going in — Sharpe ratio above 1.0, CAGR between 14 and 18%, max drawdown under 15% — specifically so I couldn't quietly redefine "success" after seeing the results.

03
What I learned

The bigger lesson wasn't the Sharpe ratio itself — it was learning to distrust a good backtest until it survives validation on data it was never allowed to see. Walk-forward optimization is slower and less flattering than a standard backtest, and that's exactly why it's the one I trust. When AlgoZen finally cleared its target under that process, it meant something different than hitting the same number on an in-sample test would have.
